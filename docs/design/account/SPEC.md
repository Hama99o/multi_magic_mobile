# Account — the policy, and deleting everything

**Status: `SPECIFIED` on the app side · `BLOCKED` on the API — the endpoint does
not exist** · his instruction, 18 Sept: *"do not forget policy page and delete
user part, and delete user deletes everything, and we should have small button
for this."*

---

## 1 · THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MOST: `delete` is not `destroy`

There is **no account-deletion endpoint.** `config/routes.rb:265` is
`resources :users, only: %i[index show update]`, and
`app/controllers/api/v1/users_controller.rb:134` holds a **commented-out**
attempt:

```ruby
# def destroy
#   if @user.delete
```

**`User` has 57 associations, nearly all `dependent: :destroy`** — loans,
expenses, incomes, budgets, recurring items, contacts, notes, documents,
api_keys, oauth tokens, trusted devices, login histories, ai keys,
mcp_tool_calls. **`@user.delete` skips callbacks and skips every one of those
dependents.** It would remove the user row and leave all of it in the database
with a dangling `user_id`: **the endpoint returns 200, the person believes they
are gone, and their loans and contacts are still there — orphaned, unowned and
unreachable.**

So the endpoint must use **`destroy`**, and that one word is the whole
difference between his instruction and its opposite. **It is a good thing this
was never finished.**

**One honest exception:** `ai_usage_events` is `dependent: :nullify` — kept
deliberately, because what the assistant cost is a billing record rather than
personal content. **That exception belongs in the policy text, not hidden in a
model file.** "Everything" with a stated exception is honest; "everything" with
an unstated one is not.

## 2 · The affordance: small entry, serious confirmation

His words: *"we should have small button for this."* Every reference agrees and
Karwan settled the same question this morning:

- **Careem** — `Sign out` and `Delete my account` are **plain rows in red**,
  below a divider, at the very bottom (`../../../Karwan/karwan-mobile/docs/design/customer/profile/references/careem-sign-out-red-row.webp`).
- **Deliveroo** — `Delete account` sits **alone in its own group**, last, with a
  trash icon.
- **Bolt Food** — `Log out` and `Delete account` both red, in a group of their
  own.

**None of them uses a button.** A low-emphasis row is harder to hit by accident
than a full-width button, which is exactly why it is the right shape for the
most destructive action in the app.

**Then the confirmation is the opposite: as heavy as the row is light.** A
screen, not a toast:

- **What goes, in a list he can read** — conversations and their files, notes,
  contacts, money records, documents, calendar events. Named, not summarised as
  *"your data"*.
- **What is kept and why** — the usage record, per §1.
- **The password, typed again.** Not a checkbox. This is the one irreversible
  action in the app and the account is reachable from a phone somebody else
  might be holding.
- **No "are you sure?" as the only guard.** The sentence that matters is
  *"this cannot be undone"*, and it is stated once, plainly.

**And it must be unmistakably different from deleting a conversation**, which
lives two rows away and is safe by construction
(`../sessions/SPEC.md`). One deletes a chat; the other deletes his life's
records. They must not look like siblings.

## 3 · The policy page

**There is no privacy policy anywhere** — not in `config/routes.rb`, not in
`app/javascript/pages`. So there is no URL to link to, and **both stores require
one**: Apple and Google each demand a reachable privacy-policy URL *and* in-app
account deletion before a listing is approved. This row is therefore not
paperwork; it is two of the conditions of shipping.

**The app renders it rather than opening a browser**, so it works on a bad
connection and does not look like leaving the app.

**The text is Hamma9900's to approve, and I will draft it from what the code
actually does rather than from boilerplate** — which is the only kind worth
having:

- **Answers are generated by DeepSeek**, so a question and the retrieved
  passages leave his server for that provider. This is the sentence most apps
  bury and the one a user most deserves.
- **Embeddings are computed locally** (`Xenova/multilingual-e5-small`), so the
  corpus itself is not shipped anywhere to be indexed.
- **Dictation uses the phone's own recogniser**, and on Android that usually
  means audio reaching Google — exactly as Chrome's does. `AI_ASSISTANT.md` §10
  already says this plainly about the web; the app says it too.
- **Uploaded files live on his server** and are deleted with their conversation.
- **The device fingerprint** is stored as a hash and exists to tell devices
  apart, not to identify a person.

## 4 · Where these two live

A small **Account** section at the bottom of the sessions sheet — the sheet is
already the app's only settings surface, and adding a tab for two rows would
cost more than it explains. Order, bottom-up: `Privacy policy` · `Sign out` ·
**`Delete account`** in red, last, alone.
